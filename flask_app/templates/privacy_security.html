Privacy & Security

WealthTrack is built to keep your portfolio data private from other users, protect sensitive credentials, and limit access to production systems. This page explains, in straightforward terms, what we store, how we protect it, and what we do not claim.

Short version: WealthTrack does not sell your portfolio data or expose it to other users. Passwords are stored as hashes, sensitive integration tokens are encrypted, and access to production systems should be restricted to site administration and support needs. WealthTrack is not currently a zero-knowledge system, which means authorized operators may be able to access stored portfolio data when necessary for maintenance, debugging, support, or legal compliance.

What WealthTrack stores

To provide portfolio tracking features, WealthTrack stores the information needed to calculate your account views and reports.

  • Account names, holdings, transactions, portfolio snapshots, and optional allocation targets
  • Login information such as username, hashed password, and verified email address
  • Optional integration data, such as Schwab connection tokens, when you choose to connect an account

How WealthTrack protects it

WealthTrack uses common web-application security practices designed to reduce risk and protect sensitive information.

  • Passwords are stored as one-way hashes, not plaintext
  • Sensitive Schwab OAuth tokens are encrypted at the database field level
  • Authenticated users can only access their own in-app portfolio data through the application
  • HTTPS, host security, and production access controls should be used to protect the live environment

What WealthTrack does not claim

WealthTrack is not currently designed as an end-to-end encrypted or zero-knowledge vault.

  • Authorized operators may be able to access stored portfolio data when needed for support or maintenance
  • Portfolio data is not currently hidden from the server in the way a true client-side encrypted system would require
  • WealthTrack does not execute trades or act as a brokerage custodian

What this means for users

The intended privacy promise is straightforward and honest.

  • Your data is private from other users
  • Your data is not sold as a product
  • Access to production data should be limited to legitimate administrative, support, security, or legal needs
  • WealthTrack should continue improving safeguards over time as the product grows

Can other users see what I own?

No. The app is built so authenticated users can only access their own data through the normal product interface.

Can WealthTrack administrators access stored portfolio data?

Potentially yes, if they have authorized access to production systems. That access should be tightly restricted and used only when necessary for support, maintenance, security, or legal compliance.

Are passwords or brokerage credentials stored in plaintext?

Passwords should never be stored in plaintext. WealthTrack stores password hashes, and sensitive Schwab OAuth tokens are designed to be encrypted in the database.

Is WealthTrack a zero-knowledge financial vault?

No. WealthTrack is a portfolio tracking application with strong privacy goals, but it is not currently an architecture where the service operator is cryptographically unable to read stored portfolio data.
