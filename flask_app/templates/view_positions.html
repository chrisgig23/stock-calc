Tax-Loss Harvesting

Tax-loss harvesting is a strategy where you sell investments that are currently worth less than you paid for them. The realized loss can offset capital gains elsewhere in your portfolio — reducing the taxes you owe for the year.

How to read this table

Unrealized Loss — the dollar amount you're currently down on this position based on your average cost per share versus today's price. No taxes are owed until you actually sell.

Est. Tax Savings — a rough estimate of the tax benefit if you harvested this loss. The 15% and 20% columns reflect the two most common long-term capital gains tax brackets. Your actual rate depends on your income and filing status.

Record Sell — logs the sale in WealthTrack. You'll still need to execute the actual trade through your brokerage.

The wash-sale rule

The IRS disallows the deduction if you buy the same or a "substantially identical" security within 30 days before or after the sale. For example, you can't sell VOO and immediately buy VOOG to stay invested. A common workaround is to buy a similar-but-different ETF for 31+ days before switching back.

When does this make sense?

It's most useful when you have realized gains elsewhere to offset, or when you're in a high tax bracket. It's generally not worth disrupting a long-term position for a small loss. Always consult a tax advisor before acting — everyone's situation is different.
